Clipboard history privacy: what to check before you install

A clipboard manager can save a great deal of time. It can also retain information you did not intend to keep. The right question is not “is it private?” but “what does it store, where, and for how long?”

By Pluks · Published September 19, 2026

Clipboard history is only as private as its retention and storage rules. Before installing a clipboard tool, understand what it captures, whether copied content leaves your device, and how you can remove it.

Five questions worth asking

  • Where is history stored? Local-only storage means your clips remain on the computer; cloud sync changes that risk model.
  • What content is captured? Check whether the tool handles text only or also images, files, and rich content.
  • How are passwords and secure fields handled? A responsible tool should document its behavior around password inputs and concealed clipboard content.
  • How long are clips retained? Choose a sensible history limit and make sure you can clear it.
  • What diagnostics are sent? Product analytics and crash reports should be separate from the contents of what you copy.

How Pluks handles copied content

Pluks keeps its clipboard history on your device. It does not transmit the text you copy. On macOS, it checks for secure text inputs and does not simulate a copy there; on supported platforms it also respects standard concealed-clipboard signals used by password managers.

Pluks can collect optional anonymous usage counts and limited crash diagnostics to help improve the product. Those diagnostics are not your clipboard content. You can turn anonymous usage reporting off in preferences. The full privacy policy describes the details, including the separate email collection used when someone requests a download.

Use history deliberately

History is most useful when it is short and searchable. It should help you recover a link or command that was replaced by the next copy—not become a permanent archive of sensitive work. Lock your computer when you step away, clear history when appropriate, and avoid installing clipboard tools on devices where you cannot accept local retention.
