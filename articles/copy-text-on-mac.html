How to copy text on a Mac—then make it automatic

The standard Mac copy shortcut is fast. The friction appears when you repeat it hundreds of times while reading, researching, replying, or moving information between apps.

By Pluks · Published September 21, 2026

To copy text on a Mac, select it and press ⌘+C; paste it later with ⌘+V. That is the reliable default. If copying is a repeated part of your day, select-to-copy can remove the copy keystroke while preserving the familiar paste step.

Start with the native workflow

Drag over a phrase, double-click a word, or use Shift and the arrow keys to make a selection. Press ⌘+C, move to the destination, and press ⌘+V. The source remains unchanged; the clipboard holds the copied item until the next copy replaces it.

When automatic copying is useful

If making a selection already means “I want this,” a helper can copy it at the end of the selection. This works well for names and dates in research, error messages in a browser, snippets in an editor, and addresses you are moving into another app. You still decide what to select; the app simply performs the ordinary copy command for you.

Pluks uses this model. On macOS it asks for Accessibility and Input Monitoring permission so it can recognize a completed selection and send the copy command. Those permissions are explained in the Pluks setup guide; Pluks avoids secure text fields.

Do not lose an earlier clip

A normal clipboard keeps only the most recent item. If a useful link or command is replaced by your next copy, a history panel saves you from retracing your steps. Pluks keeps recent clips locally; open its history with ⌘⇧V, search, and choose the item again.
